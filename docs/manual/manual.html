About this manual

This manual describes DegluTech version 0.4 (review build), a browser-based clinical workspace for speech-language pathologists (SLPs) who assess and rehabilitate swallowing, voice and speech in people treated for head and neck cancer (HNC). It is written for practising SLPs and for students working under supervision. It also serves research teams who use DegluTech to keep structured, pseudonymised study records.

Each chapter follows one part of the application. It starts with the purpose of the page, gives the procedure as numbered steps, and ends with notes on interpretation and limits. Screen names, buttons and fields are written in bold exactly as they appear on screen; keyboard keys appear as Ctrl K. All screenshots were taken with the eight fictitious demo patients that ship with the application and with two synthetic sample documents made for this manual. No real patient data were used.

Conventions

  • Chart — the record of one patient: thirteen sections, the tool suite scores, the exercise prescription and attached files.
  • Section — one of the thirteen pages of the chart (for example 6. Swallowing Assessment). A section is divided into numbered blocks.
  • Study ID — a pseudonymous code such as DT-HNC-001. Names and hospital IDs are optional and can be hidden on every printout.
  • Demo patient — fictitious teaching data, labelled Demo wherever it appears.
Clinical responsibilityDegluTech does not diagnose and does not grade severity. Every score, rating and qualifier is entered or confirmed by the clinician. Values read from documents are suggestions that the clinician ticks before they enter the chart. Alerts and readiness indicators are prompts, not clinical conclusions. Exercise programmes reach the patient view only after the clinician documents clearance and releases them. Clinical decisions remain with the treating SLP.

Chapter 1Introduction

1.1 Purpose and scope

Rehabilitation after head and neck cancer draws on many sources: the oncology summary, the radiotherapy record, the bedside swallowing examination, instrumental studies, voice measures, nutrition and airway status, patient-reported outcomes and the home programme. These are usually spread across paper case sheets, separate score forms and word-processed reports. DegluTech brings them into one structured chart that runs in a web browser. From that chart an SLP can:

  • document the patient across thirteen sections, from referral and oncology staging to a WHO-ICF functioning profile;
  • administer the EAT-10 on screen and keep a dated score history, with linked records for other instruments;
  • attach discharge summaries, swallow-study reports, voice-analysis printouts and photographs, and let DegluTech propose values from them for review;
  • prescribe exercises shown by a 3D patient model, with dose, safety stop rules and patient instructions in six languages;
  • review chart readiness, write the clinical impression and print a signed diagnostic report on the institute's letterhead.

1.3 Design principles

Records stay on the device

DegluTech has no server that stores patient data. Charts and settings are kept in the browser's local storage, and attached files in the browser's database (IndexedDB), on the computer or phone you use. Documents are read on the same device. Nothing leaves it unless you export a backup, a patient file or a report. The other side of this design is that clearing the browser's site data deletes the records, so regular backups are part of the workflow (Chapter 3).

The clinician decides

Severity, CTCAE grade, DIGEST grade, ICF qualifiers and the diagnosis are clinician-entered fields. The application never infers one from another; the report states, for example, that no automatic correlation is made between ICF qualifiers and clinical severity.

Suggestions are reviewed before use

When DegluTech reads a document, each value it finds is shown with the line of text it came from. Only ticked values are added, and values that would overwrite something already in the chart start unticked.

Safety gates for home exercise

A home programme cannot be released while a fistula or an unhealed flap or wound is present or unknown, until exercise clearance and restrictions are documented, and until an individual stop rule, a language and a dose for each exercise are recorded (Chapter 9).

Chapter 2Getting started

2.1 Requirements

  • Browser: a current version of Chrome or Edge on Windows, macOS, Linux or Android is recommended. Safari and Firefox also run DegluTech. The 3D exercise demonstrations need WebGL; when it is unavailable the drawn illustration is shown instead.
  • Hardware: a laptop or desktop is best for charting and report review. A phone or tablet is well suited to demonstrating exercises and showing the patient view.
  • Network: an internet connection is needed to open the site. Records are then kept on the device and documents are read on the device; the first time a photograph is read, the text-recognition files (about 10 MB per language) are downloaded once.
  • Pop-ups: allow pop-ups for the site; the diagnostic report opens in a new window.

2.2 Opening the workspace

  1. Open https://deglutech.pages.dev in the browser.
  2. On first use the Patients page is empty ({{fig:f-empty}}). Go to Settings first to enter your institute and clinicians (Chapter 3), then add patients (Chapter 4).
  3. To use the workspace like an app, choose Install or Add to Home screen from the browser menu where the browser offers it. It then opens in its own window with the DegluTech icon. It still needs a connection to start.

2.3 A tour of the interface

Sidebar. The panel at the top shows the open patient, their hospital and study IDs and the risk band, with Switch or add patient. Below it are Patients, Files and reports and the five section groups: Triage, Intake, Assessment, Synthesis and Plan. Each group heading shows how many of its sections are filled (for example 1/3 filled) and opens or closes when clicked; the group containing the current section opens automatically. A tick replaces the section number once every field in that section is recorded ({{fig:f-sidebar}}).

Masthead. The masthead names the page and the section group, shows the patient and the section number (Section 6 / 13), and holds the Chart completion ring: the percentage of recorded fields and the number of sections filled. Under it are Save session and the main action of the page, Review report (or Export diagnostic report on the report section). Entries are saved to the device automatically as you type; the line Saved on this device shows the last save, and Save session records a save time on demand.

Jump to a section. The search box in the masthead, or Ctrl K, opens a finder (right of {{fig:f-sidebar}}). Type part of a section name or its content (for example swallow, report or tools) and press Enter, or click the result. Esc closes it.

2.4 Using DegluTech on a phone

On a phone the sidebar is replaced by a Clinical section list at the top of every page; it contains Patients, Files and reports, the thirteen sections, the Clinical tool suite and Settings. The completion ring becomes a compact strip and form fields are shown one per row ({{fig:f-phone}}).

2.5 Privacy and data storage

  • Records are stored only in this browser on this device. Another browser, another device or a private window will not see them.
  • Clearing site data or browsing history for the site deletes the records and files. Export a backup at the end of each clinic day (Chapter 3.4).
  • Use the study ID as the main identifier for research records and switch on Hide patient names and hospital IDs on printouts and exports in Settings.
  • Check that consent covers photographs or video of the patient before attaching them, and keep exported backups in encrypted institutional storage.
Shared computersAnyone who opens the site in the same browser profile on the same computer can see the records. Use a personal browser profile protected by the operating-system login.

Chapter 3Settings

Settings hold everything that is the same for every patient: the letterhead, the clinicians who sign reports, print privacy and backups. Open Settings at the bottom of the sidebar. The four links at the top of the page (Clinic identity, Clinicians, Print & privacy, Backup & data) jump to each card.

3.1 Clinic or institute

  1. Enter the Institute or clinic name, Department, Address, Phone and Email.
  2. Add a logo with Change logo (PNG or JPG). Remove logo clears it.
  3. Changes save automatically and appear on the letterhead of every report and handout ({{fig:f-set}}).

3.2 Speech-language pathologists

  1. Click Add clinician. Enter Full name, Qualification, Designation and RCI registration number.
  2. Optionally add a scanned signature image (PNG with a white or transparent background works best); it prints above the clinician's name.
  3. Click Save clinician. Repeat for each SLP, intern or student who signs reports, and choose the default signer with the radio button.

On the report, the signing clinician can be changed per report (Chapter 10.3).

3.3 Printing and privacy

Tick Hide patient names and hospital IDs on printouts and exports to print reports with the study ID only. Use this for research records, case presentations and teaching. The preview on the card shows the letterhead as it will print.

3.4 Backup and data

  1. Export backup downloads one file (.json) with every patient, chart and setting on this device. Tick Include patient files (reports, photos, videos) to add attached files; the backup becomes much larger.
  2. Import backup reads such a file. Choose Merge with this device to add patients that are not already here, or Replace everything to make this device an exact copy of the backup.
  3. Store backups in encrypted institutional storage; a backup holds identifiable clinical data.

3.5 Demo patients for teaching

Load demo patients adds eight fictitious charts (IDs DEMO-01 to DEMO-08) covering different sites, care phases and risk levels. They are marked Demo and a banner reminds users that they are teaching data. Remove demo patients deletes all of them and leaves real records untouched.

3.6 User manual and quick start

The User manual and quick start card in Settings, and the links at the foot of every page, open this manual and the two-page quick start as PDF files.

Chapter 4Patients

The Patients page lists every chart on the device ({{fig:f-pat}}). Search by name, hospital ID, study ID or tumour site.

4.1 Adding a patient

  1. Click Add patient.
  2. Enter at least one of Full name, Hospital ID / UHID or Study ID; add Age (years), Sex and Care phase ({{fig:f-add}}). A study ID already used on this device is refused.
  3. Click Add and open chart. The chart opens at 2. Demographics and Referral.

4.2 Opening, editing, exporting and deleting

  • Open makes the patient active; their chart, files and prescription load. The open patient is marked Open.
  • Details edits the identity fields.
  • Export downloads that one patient as a backup file, for example to hand a case over to another clinician who imports it.
  • Delete asks for confirmation and then permanently removes the patient's records and attached files from the device (Delete permanently or Keep patient).

Chapter 5Charting the sections

5.1 How a section page works

Sections 2 to 10 share one layout ({{fig:f-sec}}):

  1. The section card names the section (Section 6 of 13) with Previous and Next buttons and the Clinical workflow map, which opens a grid of all thirteen sections.
  2. The Clinical documentation strip counts the recorded fields in this section, for example 7 / 17 fields recorded.
  3. In this section lists the numbered blocks; clicking one scrolls to it.
  4. Fill this section from a report, photo or file opens the document reader for this section (Chapter 8.5).
  5. Each block has a number, a title, a description, a count such as 4/4 recorded (green when complete) and a button that folds the block away. Folded blocks still print.
  6. The Documentation checklist at the end lists the outputs the section is meant to support.

Field types. Choices appear as buttons (one or several can be selected, as stated by the field), short lists as drop-down menus, measurements as number boxes with their unit, and narrative as text boxes. Fields with a helper line state the unit or convention, for example MDADI global (20–100 convention).

5.3 Recording notes

  • Staging. Record the TNM categories from the oncology record; DegluTech does not stage. Note the AJCC edition in the narrative when it matters for the study.
  • Jaw opening. Record three inter-incisal trials in millimetres. An opening of 35 mm or less is flagged as a trismus risk on the dashboard, following the criterion of Dijkstra et al. (2006).
  • Clinician-assigned grades. CTCAE dysphagia grade, DIGEST grades and ICF qualifiers are judgements that need the full clinical picture and, for DIGEST, the instrumental study itself (Hutcheson et al., 2017). Record the version used.
  • Patient-reported outcomes. MDADI (Chen et al., 2001) and VHI-10 (Rosen et al., 2004) totals are entered after scoring on the licensed forms; record the language version and who administered it.
  • Acoustics. Record the task (sustained /a/, duration), microphone distance and software in Task, recording conditions and rating source; jitter, shimmer and HNR vary with these settings. Shimmer is recorded in dB.
  • Weight. Weight change in one month of 5% or more raises a dashboard alert. Agree the nutrition plan with the dietitian.

7.1 Administering the EAT-10

  1. Click the EAT-10 card. Choose Clinician entry when you read the items aloud, or Hand to patient to give the device to the patient.
  2. For each of the ten items select 0 (no problem) to 4 (severe problem) ({{fig:f-eat}}). Bedside and Desk change the row spacing.
  3. The Total score panel shows the total out of 40 and its interpretation; while items are unanswered the total is marked provisional.
  4. Click Save to patient timeline. The total is copied to the EAT-10 field of Section 6 and added to Trend across sessions; a second save on the same day replaces the first.

Interpretation shown on screen. A total of 3 or more is abnormal (Belafsky et al., 2008). A total above 15 is associated with a higher likelihood of aspiration on instrumental examination (Cheney et al., 2015), which should prompt consideration of VFSS or FEES. The EAT-10 is a symptom screen; it does not replace an instrumental study.

7.2 Report, EMR note and handout

Under the score, three buttons open output sheets ({{fig:f-eatout}}). Report / PDF is a one-page EAT-10 report on the letterhead with item scores and history; Print / PDF prints it or saves it as PDF. EMR note is a short text note copied to the clipboard for pasting into the hospital record. Patient handout is a plain-language explanation for the patient. Esc closes the sheet.

  1. Open the patient, then Files and reports in the sidebar.

8.3 Reviewing the values

Every proposed value is shown with the line of the document it came from ({{fig:f-rev}}). Its state decides whether it starts ticked:

  • New the chart field is empty. Ticked.
  • Adds a multiple-choice field gains options, for example a second surgery type. Ticked.
  • Chart has … report says … the chart already holds a different value. Not ticked; tick it only if the document is newer and correct.
  • Already in chart the same value is already recorded. Cannot be ticked.
  • Notes (in amber) flag findings that need a decision, for example a T4 or N2 category without its sub-letter, an oropharyngeal site without p16 status, or shimmer given in per cent when the chart records dB. They are shown for information and are never added.
  1. Read each value against its source line. Untick anything doubtful.
  2. Click Add n ticked values to the chart. A message confirms how many were added. Check them in each section.
  3. Show text read from file displays the full text that was read, so you can copy anything DegluTech did not recognise. Cancel closes the review without changes.

8.4 Photographs and scans

For photographs, lay the page flat in good light, fill the frame and avoid shadows. {{fig:f-ocr}} shows values read from a photographed synthetic VFSS report. Text recognition reads printed text well; handwriting is read poorly or not at all, so typed reports give the best results. If nothing is found, DegluTech says so; retake the photograph or enter the values by hand.

8.5 Filling one section from a document

Every data section has Fill this section from a report, photo or file above its blocks ({{fig:f-inline}}). It works like the Files page, but values for the open section are listed first and the four most recent files are shown. Files added here are kept on the Files page too.

8.6 Viewing and managing files

Each file in the list shows its size, date, how it was read and how many of its values were used ({{fig:f-flist}}). View opens it in a viewer (PDF, image, video or text) with Save a copy. Find values opens the review again. Read again repeats recognition, for example after changing the photo language. The bin button deletes the file after confirmation. The line at the foot of the page shows how much browser storage is in use.

Verify every valueReading is rule-based pattern matching on the document's text. It cannot judge whether a document is current, whether a value refers to this patient or to a previous episode, or whether a report is internally inconsistent. The clinician remains responsible for each value added to the chart.

Chapter 9Exercise prescription and patient portal

9.1 Exercise library and demonstrations

Section 11 lists the eight core exercises of the home programme: Masako manoeuvre (tongue-hold swallow), effortful swallow, Mendelsohn manoeuvre, supraglottic swallow, active-assisted jaw stretch, lingual resistance press, back-tongue speech drills and the neck and shoulder range programme. Select an exercise to see its purpose, dose guidance, steps and safety notes. The Expanded exercise catalogue holds 30 further exercises for tongue, jaw, lips, neck, posture, airway, voice, speech and respiration for review; exercises are released to patients only from the core library.

Each exercise is demonstrated by a 3D patient ({{fig:f-3d}}). Choose Front view, Side view or Inside view (a schematic cut-away of the oral cavity, pharynx and larynx with the working muscles highlighted), and a Woman or Man model. Drag the model to turn it. Play, restart, half-speed and the step buttons 1, 2, 3 control the movement; holds are counted down on screen. Drawing switches to a line illustration, which is also used automatically on devices without 3D support.

9.2 Prescribing and releasing a programme

  1. Select the exercises for this patient and choose the patient's instruction language: English, ಕನ್ನಡ (Kannada), മലയാളം (Malayalam), తెలుగు (Telugu), हिन्दी (Hindi) or தமிழ் (Tamil).
  2. In Individual prescription and release, set Sessions per day and write the Clinician dose and instructions for every selected exercise ({{fig:f-rel}}).
  3. Record the individual stop and contact plan (for example stop if sharp pain, bleeding or breathing discomfort, and call the clinic).
  4. The status line names anything still missing. When nothing is missing, click Confirm review and release.

9.3 Patient portal and session log

Section 12 shows the released programme as the patient sees it, in the chosen language, with a Done button per exercise that counts the day's sessions against the prescribed number. Each exercise carries the regimen set in Section 11 ({{fig:f-portal}}). It also records today's exercises, a symptom check-in and the patient's confidence. Export session log downloads the logged days as a CSV file with study ID, exercise, sessions prescribed and logged, dose and release time. The log covers only the days logged on this device; it is not a complete adherence record and is not synchronised between devices.

Chapter 10Summary and report

10.1 Pre-publication validation

Section 13 opens with a summary card: the recorded diagnosis, three actions and a Sections 1–12 readiness grid ({{fig:f-valid}}). Each tile shows the section, its state and the number and percentage of fields recorded: green tiles with a tick are Filled · review required; amber tiles with an arrow are In progress. A badge counts the sections with missing fields. Click a tile to go to that section. Field completion shows that fields are recorded; it does not measure clinical validity.

10.2 Clinical impression

Write the SLP diagnosis, choose the Clinical severity, and enter Recommendations and the Follow-up plan. These are the clinician's own conclusions. Below them the page assembles the executive summary, the WHO-ICF table with qualifiers, and recommendation panels (strength and range-of-motion exercises, compensatory strategies and diet, red flags and emergency criteria, follow-up timeline) for review.

10.3 Sign-off

The Clinician sign-off panel shows the signature, name, qualification, designation, RCI registration number and institute of the signing clinician ({{fig:f-sign}}). When more than one clinician is set up, choose the signer from the list. DegluTech performs no electronic signature or EHR submission: sign the exported document according to your institution's procedure.

10.4 Exporting and printing

The three report actions differ in visual weight so that the main one stands out ({{fig:f-act}}):

  • Export diagnostic report (orange) opens the formatted report in a new window and the browser's print dialog. Choose Save as PDF or a printer. The report carries the letterhead, patient identity (or study ID only, if names are hidden), AJCC TNM, care phase, the executive summary, ICF table, recommendations and sign-off ({{fig:f-exp}}).
  • Preserve recorded entries stores the impression fields with the chart.
  • Print current view prints the page as shown.

If nothing opens, the browser blocked the pop-up; allow pop-ups for deglutech.pages.dev and try again.

Chapter 11Good practice and limitations

11.1 Good practice

  • Set up Settings before the first patient so that every report carries the correct letterhead and signer.
  • Use study IDs consistently for research participants and hide names on printouts.
  • Export a backup at the end of each clinic day and before clearing browser data, changing computers or updating the browser profile.
  • Attach source documents to the chart so that the origin of each value can be checked later.
  • Record instrument versions, languages, assessors and recording conditions in the fields provided; these determine whether repeated measures can be compared.
  • Review the readiness grid before exporting a report, and treat green tiles as recorded, not as verified.

11.2 Known limitations

  • This is a review build. It has not been validated as a medical device and is intended for clinical documentation support, teaching and research under supervision.
  • Records are not synchronised between devices or users. Hand over cases with patient export and import.
  • Document reading recognises the common phrasing of typed English reports; unusual layouts, tables, abbreviations and handwriting may be missed or misread. Kannada and Hindi recognition reads the script, but the value rules match English labels.
  • MDADI, VHI-10 and the Xerostomia Questionnaire are not administered on screen.
  • The 3D anatomy is schematic and the exercise demonstrations await clinician verification; patient-language instructions are draft translations awaiting native-speaker SLP review.
  • The patient session log counts sessions recorded on this device only.
